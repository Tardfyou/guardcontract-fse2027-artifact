# GuardContract External-Review Requirements and Evidence Gates

This is an English release summary of an archived author-side review checklist. Its September 20, 2026 writing targets are not official review scores, acceptance guarantees, or evidence that an unfinished experiment succeeded. The experiment design was frozen on September 19. Final experimental status is in `docs/REMAINING_EXPERIMENTS.md`; archived references to pending runs should not override it.

## Construct and paper argument

For a declared protection contract `c` and analysis scope `Omega`, Deny--Effect Consistency (DEC) rules out a feasible execution containing a matching DENY and protected commit in the same request, attempt, and protection window. The matching relation is the declared obligation; a faulty implementation cannot erase it. Same scope does not require the DENY to precede commit or imply that DENY caused the effect. Specify native DENY, protected effect, commit point, and framework-mediated scope. A missing approval or forbidden state may form a separately declared extension contract; it must not be portrayed as an emitted DENY.

Decision correctness asks whether ALLOW or DENY matches the applicable policy. Enforcement effectiveness asks whether an actual DENY prevented its matching protected commit. GuardContract primarily audits the latter. Observing DENY alone does not prove a correct verdict, and in-scope DEC does not prove whole-application safety. The conceptual distinction, the operational DEC criterion, and paired mechanism evidence form the first contribution. The other contributions are selective auditing, conditional findings in real applications, and a secondary, exploratory contract-guided repair direction. Do not rest novelty on the word `first`: Stop Means Stop already studies general control-primitive enforcement gaps. The additional task here is to recover and selectively verify application-specific obligations from existing integrations.

The research questions align with those contributions: (RQ1) with a fixed obligation and DENY, how do guard placement and binding change protected commits? (RQ2) at what coverage, correctness, and cost can GuardContract audit DEC on independent labels? (RQ3a) what can be established in the fixed real-application frame, and (RQ3b) how can audited failure paths guide limited repair? The 745-repository breadth census and 695-repository / 772-unit depth frame remain separate. Repair has no stable automatic real-repository success claim; an empty eligible repair queue produces an undefined rate.

The method is organized as **Compile -> Link -> Check**. Contract Compiler recovers source-backed candidate obligations. Execution Linker connects contracts to object identity, feasible paths, and commits. Evidence Checker releases a conclusion only under the required counterexample or scoped proof, otherwise retains an open obligation. This naming does not add an intermediate representation, solver, or semantic capability absent from the implementation. An auxiliary Repair Planner uses a failed obligation and path to consider binding/routing corrections, a public interception point, or supported deferred commit. Keep proposals, implemented patches, independent checks, and maintainer fixes distinct.

The motivating LangChain 1.4.0 `jump_to="tools"` case is a separately fixed-version example: the same HITL rejection blocks a normal route, while an alternate route writes an inert marker. It illustrates a route-specific enforcement gap but does not enter the 98-program matrix, held-out test, ecological denominator, or an automatic-detection count.

## Comparator and adjudication rules

New problem formulation does not remove the need for baselines. Within comparable inputs and tasks, report verdict-only, static-only, LLM-only, full, an explicitly adapted syntactic baseline, and frozen ablations. Verdict-only predicts blocking from an actual DENY in applicable cells; ALLOW cells are functional controls. Static-only means its frozen registration, alias, call, and path rules, not all static analysis. LLM-only directly assesses DEC within its assigned evidence budget; model predictions still require independent verification. An adapted ClawAudit-style rule set is not a run of the original tool, and unrun Semgrep/CodeQL rules must not be credited. State input, output, and guarantee differences when discussing Sefz, Semia, VIGIL, Stop Means Stop, and Commit-Time Authorization; do not manufacture comparability or omit method comparators.

Development-only numbers, if cited, retain their original denominators and scope. Historical static-only present precision 1.0 and recall 0.143, and LLM-only present recall 1.0 and precision 0.737, describe their development configurations, not final ecological performance. Historical adapted-syntactic coverage 0.217, present precision 0.4, and recall 0.143 have the same limitation. `1 - 0.737` is about 26% among predicted positives; 5/46 is the false-positive rate on that labeled-negative development subset. Do not turn any of these into final labels. The no-probe, no-binding, no-ordering/dominance, and no-cross-review/abstention interventions have their frozen meanings; removing a local check is not deleting an entire named module.

Strict labels are `VIOLATION-PRESENT`, `CONFORMANT-WITHIN-SCOPE`, `UNKNOWN`, and `EXCLUDED`, with execution health recorded separately. VP requires a mechanically checked feasible counterexample; CWS requires an explicit in-scope exclusion of all counterexamples. Citations, hashes, JSON/schema checks, textual order, reviewer agreement, or one negative run cannot replace binding and path proof. The held-out test was hidden from method development and revealed once; previously viewed items cannot be repurposed as a fresh confirmatory set. Report selective risk, coverage, abstention, failures, costs, uncertainty, and denominators on their valid labeled tasks. Retain `UNKNOWN` and `EXCLUDED`; ecological identification bounds are not confidence intervals.

The 98-program paired matrix is central RQ1 mechanism evidence, with the async extension reported separately. Controlled bidirectional DENY/ALLOW canaries support behavior labels within their eligibility gates. For real repositories, distinguish broad source-backed risk candidates from strict VP/CWS. The final strict v4 eligible set has zero decided cases, so real-repository DEC precision/recall, confirmed prevalence, and verified repair rate cannot be reported. Developer confirmations, disclosures, and repairs, if later added, are separate deduplicated impact measures and cannot substitute for detector accuracy or ecological prevalence.

Anonymized reproduction requires stable IDs/splits, source and run identities, per-item outputs, scripts, environment records, exclusion and unknown reasons, and integrity checks. The strongest paper claims must remain tied to their actual evidence tier and denominator.
